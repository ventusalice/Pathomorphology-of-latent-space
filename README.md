# Pathomorphology-of-latent-space
## Abstract
Unusual outputs from generative artificial intelligence can acquire recognizable identities and 
circulate as persistent cultural figures. This article proposes the pathomorphology of latent 
space, a comparative analytic framework examining how anomalous visual generations, 
persona-related language-model shifts, and digital occult practices emerge and stabilize. While 
retaining "latent space" as an umbrella concept, our protocol strictly distinguishes between 
multimodal embeddings, diffusion states, autoregressive activation streams, and parameter 
weights across three levels of analysis: observable model behavior and candidate 
mechanisms, human interpretive framing, and sociotechnical circulation. Applying an eight-
point protocol, we compare three cases: the visual cryptid Loab (2022), mechanistic 
interpretability studies of alignment and emergent misalignment in large language models, and 
contemporary cyber-occult experiments (Infinite Backrooms and Truth Terminal) viewed 
against 1990s precursors (TIAMAT and CCRU). Conrad Waddington’s epigenetic landscape 
and Carl Gustav Jung’s concepts of the Shadow, Persona, and enantiodromia serve as 
bounded heuristic models assessed against competing technical explanations without implying 
a shared mathematical or psychological ontology. Ultimately, we argue that digital myth-making
arises from recursive feedback among conditional model outputs, curatorial selection, and 
cultural recirculation, and formulate falsifiable research questions to test these dynamics. Our 
central distinction separates generative persistence (recurrence of specified output features 
under defined conditions), perceptual continuity (observers’ recognition of different outputs as 
the same figure), and cultural persistence (continued circulation of a named identity), where 
evidence for one does not independently establish the others.

generative AI, anomalous outputs, mechanistic interpretability, Jungian
